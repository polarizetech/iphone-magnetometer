Notch a known band:

Mains and its harmonics are the usual notch. At this sample rate a band above Nyquist folds — the notch is applied at its aliased location, and a folded band is marked ⤵.

Live strip

Gaps in the sample clock (more than 3× the median interval) are shaded; the line breaks rather than bridging them. Wall time is the phone's clock, anchored per chunk.

Spectrum

Welch PSD of the selected channel over the last 60 s, detrended, Hann, 50% overlap. Shaded bands come from BandRegistry.swift via api/bands; a band marked ⤵ is folded — it lives above Nyquist at this rate and appears here only as an alias. The dotted amber tick is the noise floor at the target: the median PSD within ±2 Hz, excluding ±0.25 Hz.

Rolling spectrogram

One column per chunk as it lands, oldest left. One dB scale across every column, stated on the canvas. Up to 360 chunks are kept in the page — at 10 s chunks that is an hour.

Crunch it on the Mac — analysis/run.py

Runs the consuming analysis (FIELDLAB_ANALYSIS) over a window of stream in its own venv and shows what it printed. The server never does the maths itself; it starts a job and reports it.

census partitions all AC variance and names what it can — anything it cannot goes to numbered U-nn lines and a broadband floor. coupling asks whether a slow band's phase predicts a carrier's envelope, through the whole surrogate ladder (S1 time-shift, S2 phase-randomised, S3 shape-matched, S4 BH-FDR) with two preconditions in front of it: a band must contain an oscillation, and the modulator must not also be in the IMU. baseline is the one to run on a clean recording with no magnet on the phone — it is what turns every "undetermined" verdict into a number.
